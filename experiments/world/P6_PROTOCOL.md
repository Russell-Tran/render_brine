# P6 protocol: can a stranger tell that a trail formed on its own?

Committed **before** any viewer sees the film, as an addendum to the pre-registration
(P6 there: "a context-free viewer, shown frames across the minute, can say that a trail
formed on its own").

**Material:** 8 frames from `showcase/ant_world.mp4` (91813a6) at 0.5, 8.5, 17, 25.5, 34,
42.5, 51 and 59.5 s, shown in time order, named neutrally. On-screen text stays in, since
it's part of the film.

**Viewer:** a fresh agent with no project context, using the same three questions as the
legibility experiment (what is this / what changes across the frames / where to look),
plus a fourth question: **"Does anything in this look planned or laid out in advance, or
does it seem to arise from what the animals do? Say what you see that tells you."**

**Grader:** a separate fresh agent with the same rubric as experiment 1, against this key:

> Ants leave a nest and wander with no path at first. After one finds a sugar pile, a
> trail (drawn blue) builds up between nest and food over time, from the ants'
> comings and goings, and by the end ants move along it in single file. The trail was
> not there at the start; it formed from the ants' behaviour.

**Pass (P6 right)** needs both:
1. **change = 2:** the viewer names the trail building up over time between nest and food
   (not merely "a blue path exists"); and
2. the answer to question 4 attributes the trail to the ants' behaviour or says it
   wasn't there at the start. The grader scores this 0 or 1 as `emergence`.

**Partly right:** change = 2 but emergence = 0, or change = 1. **Wrong:** change = 0.
